> **Bottom line**: on a typical ES day, the RTH high and low are **0.90%** apart. Half of all days fall between **0.62% and 1.34%**.
>
> Only **9.8%** of days reach 2% or more, about one day in ten. Just 2.7% reach 3% or more.
>
> <span class="takeaway">How I use it: at the open, multiply the opening price by 0.9% to estimate roughly how many points today might move. If the day has already moved more than 2%, it's a big-range day of the kind you only get about once every ten days.</span>

I measure the intraday range as a percentage: if today's RTH high and low are 80 points apart and the open is 8,000, the range is 80 ÷ 8,000 = 1%. Using percentages is the only way to compare 2010, when ES was around 1,000, with today's prices.

## A typical day: around 0.9%

Out of 3,924 trading days:

- The median is **0.90%**: half of days are smaller, half are larger.
- The middle half of days fall between **0.62% and 1.34%**.
- Days with a range under 1% make up **57.1%**; under 1.5%, **80.5%**.

## The distribution is right-skewed, not normal

The histogram rises steeply on the left and trails off in a long tail on the right. Most days are packed between 0.5% and 1.3%, but every so often there's a 5%, 8% or even 10% day.

That's why the average (1.11%) is higher than the median (0.90%): a handful of huge days pull the average up. For a "typical day", the median is the better guide.

## By year: 2017 was the calmest, 2022 the wildest

- **2017** had a median of just **0.48%**, the lowest in 16 years. A typical day moved half as much as in other years.
- **2022** had the highest median, **1.72%**. It was a bear market: even the smallest 10% of days moved at least 0.98%, more than the 16-year median.
- **2020** had a median of 1.26%, but the longest tail: the biggest 10% of days (24 of them) were all above 3.53%, and 15 of those were in March.
- 2026 through September has a median of 0.87%, about the same as the 16-year figure.

The high-volatility years line up with the market backdrop: the 2011 European debt crisis, COVID in 2020, rate hikes and the bear market in 2022, and tariffs in 2025.

## Definition and how to reproduce it

<ResearchCard title="Intraday range distribution" prompt={prompt} code={code}>

- **Data**: cleaned 1-minute ES bars, 2010-06 to 2026-09, 3,924 trading days. See [how I cleaned it](/en/posts/data-cleaning/). Half-day sessions were already removed during cleaning.
- **RTH**: 09:30–16:15 US Eastern, i.e. 1-minute bars from 09:30 to 16:14.
- **Open**: the open of the 09:30 1-minute bar.
- **Range**: (RTH high − RTH low) ÷ open.
- **Percentile**: for example, the 90th percentile is 1.99%, meaning 90% of days have a range of 1.99% or less.

</ResearchCard>

## Limitations

- **It only looks at high minus low, not direction**: up 1% and down 1% count as the same range, and where the day closes doesn't matter.
- **News days aren't separated**: FOMC, CPI and jobs-report days are counted together with ordinary days.
- **Converting to points depends on the price level**: the same 0.9% is more points when ES is trading higher.
