## Targets and trailing stops: the 16 MGC trades

Looking back at these 16 trades, I recalculated each one using only how price moved after entry. If every trade had used the same fixed rule, which rule would be best?

### A. Experiment 03 and live trades together

<RuleCompare />

<p class="table-note">Win rate = W ÷ (W + L). Breakevens are not counted. Target hit rate = W ÷ all trades.</p>

<p class="table-note">BE at 1R: once price reaches 1R, move the stop to the entry price.</p>

<p class="table-note">Stepped stop: at 1R move the stop to the entry price, at 2R to +1R, at 3R to +2R, and so on.</p>

**2R target with BE at 1R stays near the top after removing the big trades:** the 16 live trades give +0.69R. Without the biggest trade it is +0.60R. Without the three trades in the bear trend from the afternoon of 11/19 into 11/20 it is +0.38R. Leaving out the 4R target, which was added later, it ranks first either way.

**Live and in the experiment, a 2R target with BE at 1R beats a 1.5R target with BE at 1R:** live +0.69R vs +0.44R, Experiment 03 +0.16R vs +0.10R.

**BE at 1R beats leaving the initial stop in almost every case:** live and experiment, three targets, and in all six comparisons the BE version is higher. (When price comes back to entry, the trend may already be reversing, and the reason for the entry may be gone.)

**In Experiment 03 the best is a 3R target with BE at 1R:** +0.42R. Live it is +0.62R, and +0.47R combined.

**The high expectancy of "no target, stepped stop" comes from one trade:** live it is +0.81R, and the 11/19 15:24 trade alone made 7R. Without it, +0.40R.
