"Price comes back to the moving average every day" is something you hear a lot in trading, and Al Brooks says it often in his live room. I used the 5-minute ES chart from 2010 to 2026 to check three things: how many days touch the 20 EMA? When does the first touch come? Once price leaves the EMA, how many bars in a row does it stay away?

<EmaExplorer />

## Only one day in 16 years without a touch

- On 3,856 of 3,857 trading days, price touched the 20 EMA at some point during RTH: **99.97%**.
- The only exception was **2024-01-08**: a very strong bull day that rallied from the open to the close, with every 5-minute bar's low above the EMA.
- Every year except 2024 is at 100%.

## Al Brooks remembered that day too

I found two passages about it in transcripts of Al Brooks' 2024 live sessions:

- **2024-03-21**: he said 99.9%, or really 99.99%, of days touch the moving average at some point, and he could remember only one day that stayed above it all day without a single touch, "about a month or two ago".
- **2024-01-09**: he opened by saying the previous day was unusual, the first time in his 37 years of trading that he had seen the market go a whole day without touching the moving average.

Both passages refer to January 8, 2024. The data also has only that one day in 16 years, and 99.97% falls right between his 99.9% and 99.99%. What Al said matches the data.

## When the first touch comes: not necessarily at the open

| By bar | Time | Already touched |
|---|---|---:|
| Bar 1 | 9:35 | 17.3% |
| Bar 6 | 10:00 | 40.5% |
| Bar 12 | 10:30 | 60.6% |
| Bar 18 | 11:00 | 72.7% |
| Bar 24 | 11:30 | 81.7% |
| Bar 36 | 12:30 | 91.6% |

On many days the open is already near the EMA, so touching it on the first bar is common. Could most of the "touches" just be bar 1? No: bar 1 accounts for only about 17%. **On more than 80% of days, price leaves the EMA after the open and then comes back to touch it.**

But how long it takes to come back varies a lot: 40% of days still haven't touched it by 10:30, and 10% by midday. So "price will touch the EMA today" is right, but "price will come back to the EMA right after the open" is not.

## Away from the EMA: a streak usually lasts 3 bars

When a whole bar is above the EMA (low above the EMA) or below it (high below the EMA), price is "away from the EMA"; consecutive bars like this form a streak.

**How streaks are counted**: each day, start from 5-minute bar 1. Consecutive bars above the EMA form one streak above; once a bar touches the EMA or moves to the other side, the streak ends, and going back above starts a new streak. A single bar above, followed by a bar that touches the EMA, still counts as a streak (of length 1). A streak still running at the close is cut off there, and counting starts over at the next open. Streaks below work the same way.

For example, a day might look like this (an illustration, not real data):

| Bars | Relative to the EMA | Counted as |
|---|---|---|
| 1–3 | Touch | — |
| 4–9 | Above | Streak 1 above (6 bars) |
| 10 | Touch | — |
| 11–12 | Above | Streak 2 above (2 bars) |
| 13–20 | Below | Streak 1 below (8 bars) |
| 21 | Touch | — |
| 22 on | Above | Streak 3 above |

That day has 3 streaks above and 1 below. Adding up the streaks on all 3,857 days gives these totals:

- **Streaks above**: 18,719, about 4.9 a day.
- **Streaks below**: 16,191, about 4.2 a day.
- 34,910 in total, about 9 a day. All the percentages in the chart are based on these streaks. For example, "at least 10 bars in a row, above 21.9%" means 21.9% (4,103) of the 18,719 streaks above lasted at least 10 bars.

As for how long these streaks last:

- The median length is **3 bars**.
- **93.5%** of streaks are 20 bars or shorter.
- Streaks above the EMA last longer: 8.3% of streaks above reach 20 bars or more, versus 5.9% below; at 40 bars or more, 1.5% above versus 0.6% below. ES rose for most of these 16 years, and uptrends can "hang" above the EMA for longer.

**A streak of 20+ bars without touching the EMA is rare as a single streak, but not rare by day**: a day has many streaks, and it only takes one long enough, so **56.9%** of days have at least one streak of 20 bars or more.

Al Brooks calls this "20 gap bars": when price goes about 20 or more bars without touching the moving average, the first return to it usually draws buyers near the moving average (sellers in a downtrend). This post only counts how often it happens; it doesn't test what comes next. That's for a separate study.

## Definition and how to reproduce it

<ResearchCard title="20 EMA" prompt={prompt} code={code}>

- **Data**: cleaned 1-minute ES bars, 2010-06 to 2026-09. See [how I cleaned it](/en/posts/data-cleaning/).
- **20 EMA**: 20-period exponential moving average of the closes of 5-minute RTH bars (09:30–16:15 US Eastern), alpha = 2 / 21. RTH bars only, carried from day to day, no overnight session.
- **5-minute slots with no trades**: during the March 2020 circuit-breaker halts, 7 five-minute bars had no trades; the EMA skips them and stays unchanged.
- **Contract rolls**: on the first day of a new contract the EMA restarts and that day isn't counted (66 days, plus the first day of the data, which takes the sample from 3,924 days to 3,857).
- **Touch**: low ≤ EMA ≤ high, using the EMA's value after the bar closes, i.e. the value drawn on the chart.
- **Away from the EMA**: low > EMA (above) or high < EMA (below).
- **Streak**: consecutive bars all above the EMA (or all below) form a streak, which ends when a bar touches the EMA or moves to the other side. A single bar counts as a streak. Counting restarts at each open, and a streak still running at the close is cut off there.
- **Streaks above / below**: the number of streaks above (below) the EMA, added up over all days. Over 16 years: 18,719 above and 16,191 below, 34,910 in total, about 9 a day. The note under the chart updates when you change the years.
- **Streak share**: the percentage for "at least L bars in a row" in the chart = streaks of length ≥ L ÷ all streaks on the same side. It's counted by streak, not by day.
- **By day**: a day counts if it has at least one streak of 20 bars or more; 56.9% of days do.

</ResearchCard>

## Limitations

- **It only checks whether price touched the EMA, not what happened next**: whether price bounces off the EMA or slices through isn't measured here.
- **The EMA uses RTH bars only**: an EMA on the 24-hour chart (including the overnight session) would sit somewhere else at the open, and the share of days touching it on bar 1 would differ.
- **Streaks are cut off at the close**: a streak that runs into the close ends there and counting restarts the next day, so a long trend spanning two days is split into two streaks.
