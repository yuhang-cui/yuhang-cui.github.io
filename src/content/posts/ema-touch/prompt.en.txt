I have a CSV of cleaned 1-minute ES futures bars (Databento's ES.c.0 continuous contract, with columns ts_event, instrument_id, high, low, close; timestamps in UTC). Please write Python code that measures how price touches the 20 EMA on the 5-minute RTH chart. Rules:
1. Convert timestamps to US Eastern time (US/Eastern), handling daylight saving time automatically. Use RTH only, 09:30–16:15, and build 5-minute bars from the 1-minute bars, 81 a day (09:30–09:35 is bar 1). If a 5-minute slot has no trades at all (for example during the March 2020 circuit-breaker halts), that bar is empty.
2. 20 EMA = 20-period exponential moving average of 5-minute closes, alpha = 2 / 21: EMA = alpha × this bar's close + (1 − alpha) × the previous bar's EMA. RTH bars only, carried from day to day, no overnight session. Skip empty bars and leave the EMA unchanged.
3. On a day when the contract rolls (instrument_id changes), restart the EMA from that day's first close and leave the day out of the stats, because old and new contract prices don't line up; same for the first day of the data.
4. Touch: the EMA (its value after this bar closes, i.e. the value drawn on the chart) sits between the bar's high and low, i.e. low ≤ EMA ≤ high.
5. Print: the sample size, the share of days that touched the EMA, the share that touched it on bar 1, and which days never touched it; the cumulative share of days whose first touch came by bars 3, 6, 12, 18, 24 and 36; and the share of days touched in each year.
6. Away from the EMA: the whole bar is above the EMA (low > EMA) or below it (high < EMA). Consecutive bars like this form one streak, reset at each open. Print the total number of streaks, the number above and below, the average per day, the median length, the share of streaks 20 bars or shorter, the share of streaks above and below lasting at least 10, 20 and 40 bars (as a share of all streaks on the same side), and the share of days with at least one streak of 20+ bars.
Please add comments to every step explaining why it's done that way.
