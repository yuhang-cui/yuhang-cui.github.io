## Summary

<DataTabs>
<div class="dt-panel">

- **Where the data comes from**: I bought it from [Databento](https://databento.com). The dataset is CME's official feed, **CME Globex MDP 3.0** (code `GLBX.MDP3`). The instrument is the ES (E-mini S&P 500 futures) continuous contract `ES.c.0`, **1-minute bars**, from June 2010 to September 2026.
- **The prices themselves are clean**: no missing values, no duplicate timestamps, no impossible highs or lows, and every price sits on the 0.25 tick.
- **The real problem is which days you can't use.** I dropped three kinds of days:
  1. **Days around a contract roll** (66 rolls): old and new contract prices don't line up, and on expiration Friday there's no RTH data at all;
  2. **Holidays and half days** (128 days, 5 of which overlap with a roll): the stock market is closed and only futures trade, or the market closes early at 13:00;
  3. **Days with bad data** (14 days): a big chunk missing from the feed, or an overnight session stretched over several nights by a holiday.
- **Result**: **3,924** trading days left, losing only about 2% of full trading days. Every study on this site uses this data.

</div>
<div class="dt-panel">

- **Where the data comes from**: the same dataset as ES. The instrument is the GC (COMEX gold futures) continuous contract `GC.v.0`, **1-minute bars**, from June 2010 to November 2026.
- **The prices themselves are clean**: no missing values, no duplicate timestamps, no impossible highs or lows, and every price sits on the 0.1 tick.
- **The real problem is still which days you can't use.** Again three kinds:
  1. **Days around a contract roll** (81 rolls, two days each): the roll happens in the middle of the Asian session, so the roll day mixes two contracts, and the day before it the old contract has almost no trades;
  2. **Holidays and half days** (119 days, 12 of which overlap with a roll): gold trades as usual on US holidays but closes in the early afternoon;
  3. **Days with bad data** (11 days): data starts hours after the open, or a big chunk is missing in the middle.
- **Result**: **3,945** trading days left, losing about 4% of full trading days. That's twice the ES loss, because gold rolls every two months.

## Where the data comes from

![The CME Globex MDP 3.0 dataset page on Databento](./databento-glbx-mdp3.png)

CME Globex MDP 3.0 is the Chicago Mercantile Exchange's official market data feed. Every futures and options contract that trades on Globex comes through it. Databento has this data from June 6, 2010 onward and charges by usage. The 1-minute bars cost me about $20 each for ES and for gold.

### Why a "continuous contract"

<DataTabs>
<div class="dt-panel">

ES isn't one contract. There's **one for every quarter**: ESZ6 expires in December 2026, ESH7 in March 2027, and so on. Each contract is the front month only for the few months before it expires.

To study 16 years of price action, you have to stitch these contracts together end to end. `ES.c.0` is the continuous contract Databento stitches for you: it always follows the nearest expiry and rolls to the next one after expiration. That automatic roll turns out to be the biggest trap.

</div>
<div class="dt-panel">

Gold has a contract for every month, but only the even months actually trade: February, April, June, August, October and December. To study 16 years, you again have to stitch them together.

Databento offers two ways to stitch: `c.0` follows the nearest expiry, and `v.0` follows whichever contract has the most volume. For gold, `c.0` won't do, because it would land on the odd-month contracts that nobody trades, with only a handful of bars a day. So I used `GC.v.0`. When and how it rolls is the biggest trap below.

</div>
</DataTabs>

## The real problem: which days you can't use

### 1. Around contract rolls

<DataTabs>
<div class="dt-panel">

`ES.c.0` only switches to the new contract **on the Sunday evening after expiration Friday**. But the old contract stops trading at 9:30 a.m. on expiration Friday. So:

- **There's no RTH data at all on expiration Friday**;
- When the code looks for Monday's overnight session, it searches back to Thursday evening and splices "Thursday evening (old contract) + Sunday evening (new contract)" into one overnight session. Old and new contracts usually differ by 10 to several dozen points, once by as much as 224 points, so the spliced overnight high and low are fake.

**What I did**: for every roll, delete everything from Thursday evening to Monday's close after the roll. Monday is dropped entirely; Tuesday is kept in full.

One special case: in June 2026, expiration day fell on Juneteenth (Friday 6/19, market closed), so expiration moved up to Thursday and the contract rolled that same evening. I handled it separately.

</div>
<div class="dt-panel">

`GC.v.0` rolls at **midnight UTC**, which is 19:00 Eastern in winter and 20:00 in summer. Gold opens at 18:00, so the roll lands an hour or two into the Asian session. Over 16 years it rolled 81 times, and on 54 of those days the first two hours are the old contract and the rest is the new one, all inside the same trading day. The gap between the two was only a dollar or two in the early years, but 30 to 60 dollars in the last three years, once as much as $61.7 (1.9%). On a 1-minute chart that's a big gap that never happened.

The other problem is the day before the roll. Rolling by volume sounds like it should switch exactly when volume moves over, but in practice it's a day late: on the day before the roll, the old contract has only about 4% of normal volume, and half the minutes have no trades, so no bars. One more day back, volume is about a third of normal and the bars are mostly there.

### 2. Holidays and half days

<DataTabs>
<div class="dt-panel">

128 days. 5 of these fall inside a roll's deletion window and are already gone with the roll, so the funnel below counts only 123 for this group.

- **Stock market closed, futures open for half a day**: Martin Luther King Jr. Day, Presidents' Day, Memorial Day, July 4, Labor Day, Thanksgiving, Juneteenth. The stock market isn't open at all on these days, and volume is only 1–10% of normal;
- **Stock market closes early at 13:00**: the day after Thanksgiving, July 3, Christmas Eve. Only half a session, not comparable with a full trading day.

</div>
<div class="dt-panel">

On US holidays gold doesn't dry up the way ES does. It opens at 18:00 as usual and closes early in the afternoon, with roughly 40% of normal volume. The closing time has changed a few times:

- **Martin Luther King Jr. Day, Presidents' Day, Memorial Day, Juneteenth, July 4, Labor Day, Thanksgiving**: closed at 13:15 in 2010–2013, 13:00 in 2014–2021, and 14:30 from 2022 on;
- **The day after Thanksgiving and Christmas Eve**: always 13:45.

Good Friday, Christmas and New Year's Day are full closures. Those days simply aren't in the raw data, so there's nothing to delete.

**What I did**: drop every trading day whose last bar is earlier than 16:00. That's 119 days, 12 of which are already inside a roll's deletion window, so the funnel below counts only 107 for this group.

</div>
</DataTabs>

<div class="dt-panel">

11 days:

| Problem | Days | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Data starts hours after the open | 5 | 2014-08-25: first bar at 22:00; 2020-02-28: data only resumes at 03:00, the same feed outage as ES |

| First day of the dataset | 1 | 2010-06-07 starts at 20:00 |

Another 4 regular trading days are missing entirely (2012-09-12 and 2014-09-23 to 25), the same source problem as ES. A gold trading day is self-contained and borrows nothing from the day before, so the day after a missing day doesn't need to be dropped the way it does for ES.

## Limitations

<DataTabs>
<div class="dt-panel">

- **The week before expiration uses the expiring contract**, whose volume is only about 1/4 of the new one. It barely matters for price structure, but exclude those days when you study volume;
- **Prices are not back-adjusted**, so price jumps at each roll. Percentages are fine, but don't compare absolute point moves across different years;
- **Data before May 21, 2017 comes from the exchange's historical files**, without Databento's own capture timestamps. It hardly matters for 1-minute bars, but it's worth knowing;
- **23 days are missing a few RTH bars**: 19 are in expiration week, when the old contract trades thinly and some minutes have no trades, so no bar (1–9 bars short per day); the other 4 are the March 2020 circuit breakers, when trading halted for 15 minutes (13–14 bars short per day). The prices are fine; just those few minutes are missing;
- **46 days have incomplete overnight data**. RTH is normal on those days, so I didn't drop them. Filter them out separately if you study the overnight session.

</div>
<div class="dt-panel">

- **Two days before a roll, volume is only about a third of normal**, and the day is short by 100–500 bars, mostly in the Asian session. There are 78 such days and I kept them. When you study volume or the Asian session, filter them out with a bar count of 1,300 or more;
- **Prices are not back-adjusted**, so price jumps at each roll: a few dollars in the early years, $30–60 in the last three. Percentages are fine, but don't compare absolute prices across months, and when you compare "yesterday and today", check that both days have the same `instrument_id` first;
- **From September 21, 2015 the close moved from 17:15 to 17:00**, so a day went from 1,395 bars to 1,380. Anything counted in bars or "N minutes before the close" needs to handle the two periods separately;
- **Six pre-holiday days in 2010–2011 closed at 16:15** (2010-07-02, 09-03, 10-08, 11-24, 12-31 and 2011-01-14), an hour short. I didn't drop them;
- **Data before May 21, 2017 comes from the exchange's historical files**, same as ES;
- **London and New York switch to daylight saving on different dates**, so for two weeks a year "08:00 London" isn't "03:00 New York". Keep that in mind if you split the European session at the London open.

</div>
</DataTabs>
