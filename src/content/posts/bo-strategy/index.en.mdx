## 1. 📜 Strategy rules

### How I think about breakouts

1. **A breakout is a trend bar**: one or a run of big bull or bear bars, with price crossing the previous bar, a prior high or low, a trend line, the EMA or the edge of a pattern.
2. **The stronger it is, the more likely there is follow-through**: the bigger the body and the closer the close to the extreme, the more likely a second and third leg. Breakouts with real follow-through are a minority of bars; most of the time the market is in a channel or a trading range.
3. **The follow-through bars decide whether it's real**: the next bar or few bars after the breakout should be in the same direction and close strong; if a reversal bar follows right away, the breakout will probably fail and turn into a trading range or a reversal.
4. **Where the second leg comes from**: the counter-trend side is trapped and has to stop out, the with-trend side missed it and has to chase, and both sets of orders push out the second leg. If the counter-trend side can't produce a strong reversal within 5–10 bars of the pullback, it gives up and the trend resumes.

### Conclusions

1. **BO makes its money on a few big swings**: in most replay tables the best 3 trades contribute about 60–77% of the profit; the rest are mostly small wins and small losses.
2. **Live, it turned into small wins and big losses**: the live average win is 11.5 points, the average loss 16.5, so losses are bigger than wins. In replay I could hold the big swing; live I couldn't, and the one-line reviews keep saying "could have taken a bigger swing".
3. **Without the big trades it barely makes money**: live, without the best 3 trades, the total is −36.3 points.

### Conclusions

1. **Almost all the profit comes from limit orders and trailing stops**: in February those two methods won all 10 trades, +216.5 points combined, more than the whole month's +174.8.
2. **Strong H1 & L1 exits are for losing less**: in February they were negative combined, but mostly they got out before a loss grew, and the notes say "avoided doubling the loss". By March this exit won 5 of 7, so the execution was improving.
3. **Limitation**: the exit method and the result are tied together; a stop out is always a loss, and a trailing stop mostly happens on trades that have already run. So this table only shows which methods help me hold the big trades, not that "choosing this exit makes money". Each type has only 1–7 trades.

1. **The third push is the only theme that loses money overall**: "entered on the third push" or "didn't exit at the third push" is the most repeated lesson in BO. Later I moved to Wedge, which trades exactly the reversal after a third push; you could say that lesson became a strategy.
2. **BO inside a TR is wasted effort**: 16 trades, total close to zero.
3. **Trailing stop and limit are the two keywords that make money**, consistent with the exit section above.

## ⚖️ Strengths and weaknesses

<section class="rc policy">
<div class="rc-panel rc-def">

<p class="policy-title">Strengths</p>

1. **Clear direction**: only trade with a strong trend bar that has already formed, no picking tops and bottoms; the BO grade and the context grade give explicit scoring criteria.
2. **Big profit when you catch the swing**: replay average wins are 15–30 points, and the best few trades run to dozens of points.
3. **Decent win rate**: 52–79% in replay, 65% live.
4. **The grading works**: grade B is the worst in every table, and context S averages +21.7 points per trade. Only taking the top grades would improve the results a lot.
5. **It trains exits**: limit targets, trailing stops and exiting at the third push carry over to other strategies.

<p class="policy-title">Weaknesses</p>

1. **Wide stop**: the initial stop averages 26–29 points (about $130–145 per MES contract). My Wedge stop is now about 7–8 points.
2. **Little profit relative to risk**: only 0.29× the initial risk per trade (Jan–Mar 2026 replay), and only 0.06× live.
3. **Dependent on a few big trades**: live, without the best 3 trades, it loses.
4. **Can't hold them live**: live average win 11.5 points, average loss 16.5; trailing the stop too tight and entering before the bar closed were the two core problems in the April live trades.
5. **Entry conditions are loose**: what counts as a "strong" breakout is a judgement call, so grade B trades slip in; a loose entry means a wide stop.
6. **Third pushes and TRs lose the most**: 19 third-push trades total −35.8 points, and BO inside a TR is wasted effort.

## 🧭 Lessons carried over to Wedge

BO's live expectancy is only +1.7 points, with a wide stop, so I've stopped using it. These lessons continue in Wedge.

<section class="rc policy">
<div class="rc-panel rc-def">

<p class="policy-title">1. When a third push appears, exit or tighten the stop</p>

1. Don't chase on the third push.
2. If already in a position and the third push shows weakness, or a counter-trend signal bar comes with it, exit actively instead of waiting for the stop.

<p class="policy-title">2. No entries inside a trading range</p>

1. Don't chase breakouts inside a TR.
2. When the market turns into a TR, place a limit to take profit near the top of the range.

<p class="policy-title">3. When the counter-trend side gets a disappointing bar, move the stop</p>

1. A surprise bar or an outside bar means the stop must be moved; make it a reflex.
2. But don't trail too tight; leave the trend room.

<p class="policy-title">4. Only grade A and above</p>

1. Grade B trades lose over time; don't take them.

</div>
</section>
