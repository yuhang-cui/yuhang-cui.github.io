Gold trades around the world. GC opens at 18:00 ET and closes at 17:00 ET the next day, with a one-hour break.

For a trader on US Eastern time, most of that day happens overnight: Asia trades through the evening and Europe opens at 03:00. The US morning is still where most of the range is made.

All clock times here are ET. Shanghai has no daylight saving, so its open is 21:00 ET in summer and 20:00 ET in winter; the text uses summer time. The charts default to the current season.

This post uses 16 years of 1-minute data to answer four questions: how far gold moves in a day, when each market opens, where in the day the range is made, and when the day's high and low are made.

## How the range is spread across the day

Take each 5-minute bar's high minus low, divide it by that day's full high-to-low range, then average by time of day. That gives one 24-hour curve. Every bar has the same denominator, so different times of day can be compared directly. The average 5-minute bar is 5.6% of the day's range.

The shares of separate bars or windows cannot be added up. Price goes back and forth, the windows overlap in price, and the total comes to far more than 100%.

The baseline below is "any random hour": take one hour starting anywhere in the day, and on average it covers 20.5% of the day's range. Only a window above that is busier than usual.

<GoldOpens />

The bar size can be changed to 15 minutes, 30 minutes or 1 hour. The 5-minute view shows the exact moment of a spike. The 1-hour view shows whether that stretch of the day actually went somewhere.

- **The US morning has the biggest range**: from 08:00 to 11:00 ET every 5-minute bar is around 10% of the day's range, nearly twice the average. The biggest is the 08:30 data bar at 16%, and the 09:30 stock-open bar is 12%. The first hour after the New York open covers 39.0% of the day's range, against 20.5% for any random hour.
- **Shanghai is big for the opening bar only**: the 21:00 ET bar is 9%, and the next bar is back near average. The first hour covers 21.7% of the day's range, about the same as any random hour.
- **The Europe opening bar is 7.5%** (03:00 ET). The hour after it covers 22.6%, only slightly above normal.
- **Where the range is big, the net move is big too**: each hour's close-to-open move is about 46% of that hour's high-to-low range, and this barely changes across the 24 hours (45%–50%). So the hours with the biggest range are also the hours most likely to produce a real leg.

### What changed since 2024

Gold has been in a bull market since 2024, and more people are watching it. Here are the same numbers for the 687 trading days of 2024–2026 alone:

<div class="exp-table">

| | First hour as a share of the day's range | Day's high or low made in that hour |
|---|---:|---:|
| Shanghai | 20.9% → **25.7%** | 10.8% → **14.1%** |
| Europe | 23.1% → **20.6%** | 8.1% → **4.5%** |
| New York | 39.3% → **37.5%** | 20.4% → **21.1%** |
| Any random hour | 20.5% → 20.3% | 8.3% → 8.0% |

</div>

<p class="table-note">Left of the arrow is 2010–2023, right is 2024–2026. Click "Pre/post 2024" on the chart to see both curves.</p>

- **Shanghai**: the first hour went from 20.9% to 25.7% of the day's range. It used to match any random hour and is now a quarter above it. The whole Asia session (18:00 ET to the London open) went from 48.4% to 56.3%.
- **Europe**: down from 23.1% to 20.6%, now the same as any random hour. Only 4.5% of days make their high or low in that hour.
- **New York**: 39.3% and 37.5%, basically unchanged. It is still the biggest stretch of the day.

## When the day's high and low are made

Same time axis, a different measure: the chance that the day's high or low falls in a given bar. It answers when the day's extremes usually get set. The default is 1-hour bars, and the average bar is about 8.7%. The high and the low can also be viewed separately.

<GoldOpens part="hilo" />

- **The opening hour is the highest** (18:00 to 19:00 ET, 19%–23%): on a one-way day, the start of the day is one of the extremes.
- **The US morning** (08:00 to 11:00 ET) is 17%–19% per hour, twice the average bar.
- **The late Asia session is the lowest** (23:00 to 02:00 ET), at 2%–3% per hour.
- **Viewed separately, the high and low lines nearly overlap**: they are spread across the hours in about the same way.
- **By session**: the high is made in the Asia, Europe and US sessions 33% / 13% / 54% of the time, and the low 34% / 13% / 53%.
- **Since 2024**: lows made in the Asia session rose from 32% to 43%. Highs made in the last hour before the close (16:00 to 17:00 ET) rose from 3.6% to 8.9%.

## Checks and method

**The spike on each opening bar is caused by that market's open**. When Shanghai is closed, the 09:00 Beijing spike disappears. When Europe's clock shifts, the spike moves with it. When US stocks are closed, the 09:30 spike disappears. After the open, Shanghai's effect lasts about two hours, and New York stays above its holiday level all morning.

<div class="tt-panel">

- **Data**: cleaned 1-minute GC bars, 2010-06 to 2026-11, 3,945 trading days. See [how I cleaned it](/en/posts/data-cleaning/). A trading day runs from 18:00 ET the previous day to 17:00 ET.
- **Daily range**: the day's high minus its low. The percentage uses the last close before the open as the denominator. The tables show medians.
- **Curve**: each bar's high minus low ÷ that day's high minus low, averaged by ET clock time. The Shanghai open is fixed in Beijing time, which falls an hour apart on the ET clock between winter and summer, so winter and summer are drawn separately.
- **Share of the day's range**: the high minus low inside a window ÷ that day's high minus low. Windows overlap in price, so the shares add up to more than 100%.
- **Net move**: a bar's close minus its open, as an absolute value.
- **Day's high or low made here**: at least one of the day's high and low falls inside the window. If the same price prints more than once, the first print counts.
- **High/low curve**: the combined line is the share of days on which at least one of the day's high and low falls in the bar, and the bars add up to about 200%. Counted separately, the high and the low each add up to 100%.
- **Any random hour**: start a window every 15 minutes through the day, take the same length, and average the results. Only a window above it is busier than usual.
- **Control charts**: the y-axis is the 5-minute range divided by that day's average bar, which is the main chart on a different scale. The x-axis is aligned on the local opening time.
- **Control groups**: China's closed days are the first to sixth day of the Lunar New Year and October 1–7 (145 days). The out-of-step weeks are the weeks when the US and Europe have switched clocks on different dates (274 days). US stock holidays are the US holidays on which gold closes early (87 days).
