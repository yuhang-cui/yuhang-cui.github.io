I have a CSV of cleaned 1-minute gold futures (GC) bars (Databento's GC.v.0 continuous contract, with columns ts_event, instrument_id, high, low; timestamps in UTC). Please write Python code that measures whether today breaks yesterday's high or low. Rules:
1. Convert timestamps to US Eastern time (US/Eastern), handling daylight saving time automatically. A gold trading day runs from 18:00 the previous day to 17:00, with no split between RTH and an overnight session. Bars after 18:00 belong to the next trading day. For example, the session that opens Sunday at 18:00 belongs to Monday.
2. Yesterday's high / low = the highest high / lowest low of all bars in yesterday's trading day. Today's high / low works the same way.
3. "Yesterday" is the most recent gold trading day before today: Monday to Friday, minus the three full-day closures, New Year's Day, Good Friday and Christmas. Build them with pandas' built-in holiday rules. Monday's yesterday is the previous Friday.
4. If that "yesterday" isn't in my data (it was removed during cleaning, for example a half day on a US holiday or a contract roll day), skip today; don't search further back. If the two days have different instrument_id values (different contracts), skip as well.
5. Upside breakout: today's high is strictly greater than yesterday's high (equal doesn't count, i.e. at least 1 tick of 0.1 above). Downside breakout: today's low is strictly less than yesterday's low.
6. Put each day into one of four outcomes: up only, down only, both sides, neither. Print the sample size, the share of each outcome, the share of days that broke at least one side, the share of days the high was broken and the share the low was broken, and a table by year.
7. Add a switch for the New York session only (09:30–16:00 US Eastern). When it's on, both today's and yesterday's high and low use only the bars in that window. Other rules unchanged.
Please add comments to every step explaining why it's done that way.
