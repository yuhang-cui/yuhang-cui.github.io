> **Hypothesis**: with the stop beyond the signal bar (= 1R) and a mechanical, fixed 2R target, the expectancy is positive.
>
> **Result**: of 30 trades, 12 hit the 2R target, 14 hit the initial stop and 4 were closed at the end of the day. Win rate 40%, above the 33.3% breakeven rate; **+0.33R** per trade on average.
>
> <span class="takeaway">In one sentence: a mechanical fixed 2R target has positive expectancy; but with 30 trades the expectancy has an error of about ±0.25R, so it is only "probably positive", not yet stable.</span>

### Conclusions

1. **Strong signal bars clearly beat weak ones**: the 23 strong trades made +17R in total; all 7 weak ones were stopped out.
2. **strong pin bars look much better than strong trend bars**: about 22 points higher for the 2R target (64% vs 42%), about 12 points higher for 3R (45% vs 33%), about 24 points higher for 1R (91% vs 67%), and 41 points higher for 1.5R (91% vs 50%). Average +0.97R vs +0.53R: 0.44R more per trade, about 1.8×. Limitations:
   1. Each group has only 11–12 trades, so the gap isn't statistically significant yet;
   2. The two show up in different places: 7 of the 12 trend bars were in wedge flags, mostly inside trading ranges; pin bars were more often at the edge of a range or at an overshoot. Part of the gap may come from location, not the bar shape.
3. **weak pin bars rarely reach 2R**: 6 in the experiment plus 5 live, and none of the 11 reached 2R; but 3 of the live ones reached 1R. If you take them, keep the target close (1R–1.5R). Only 11 trades, so treat it as a hint.
4. **After a strong pin bar, the entry bar's follow-through matters too**: of 7 live trades, the 3 with a good trend bar or acceptable follow-through as the entry bar all won, and the 4 with a counter bar all lost (price got no further than 0.17–0.31R). In the experiment, the 2 strong pin bars followed by a strong counter bar went 1 win, 1 loss.
5. **Each signal bar has a different best target**: for strong pin bars it's 1.5R (+1.27R, above +0.97R at 2R); for strong trend bars it's 3R (+0.70R), and the closer the target the worse; weak pin bars are negative at every target. Limitations:
   1. 3R often means holding for half a day or more, and there are often deep pullbacks after 2R, so the simulation is optimistic and hard to execute;
   2. 1.5R's edge comes from turning 3 stop-outs into wins, and in those 3 trades 1.5R happened to sit near the top of a range or the start of the rally;
   3. A better approach may be to set the target from structure (range edges, the start of the rally, prior highs and lows) and the stop size rather than a fixed multiple. That is a candidate hypothesis for Experiment 02.

## 2. 🚦 Entry bar

### Definition

<p class="table-title">Entry bar definitions (for a long; reverse for a short)</p>

<div class="exp-table">

| Grade | What counts |
|:---|:---|
| Good trend bar | a. Bull bar with a relatively large body for that moment<br/>b. Short or no upper tail<br/>c. A long lower tail is fine (buying pressure)<br/>d. Closes well beyond the previous bar's close |
| Acceptable follow-through | a. Bull bar with a medium-to-small body<br/>b. Bull bar with a large body but a long upper tail (broke out, still closed fairly high) → one grade below "good trend bar" |
| Doji | a. Very small body, bull or bear<br/>b. Small body, upper and lower tails about the same<br/>c. Small body, long lower tail, short upper tail<br/>d. Bull bar, but with a small body compared with the big bull bars around it<br/>e. Small bull bar with a longer upper tail → one grade below "acceptable follow-through" |
| Mild counter bar | a. Bear bar with a modest body<br/>b. Small body but a longer upper tail, clear selling pressure → one grade below Doji<br/>c. Bear bar with a medium body and a longer lower tail → still a mild counter bar |
| Strong counter bar | a. Bear bar with a relatively large body for that moment<br/>b. A strong pin bar in the opposite direction (long upper tail, closes near the low) |

</div>

<p class="table-note">Body size is judged against the bars around it at the time, not a fixed number of points. For a long, the upper tail = selling pressure = the counter-trend tail; the lower tail = buying pressure.</p>

### Conclusions

1. **The entry bar predicts the early follow-through best, not how far the trade goes in the end**: the gap between a good trend bar and a Doji shrinks as the target gets farther: 62 points at 1R, 45 at 1.5R, 25 for the 2R target, and only 8 at 3R. One reason is that a good trend bar often carries price close to 1R by itself.
2. **Each entry bar has a different best target**: all 6 good trend bars reached 1R, so 1R and 1.5R both beat 2R, and moving to breakeven at 1R costs nothing; acceptable follow-through is about the same at every target, with 1.5R and 3R slightly better; Doji is around zero or negative at every target.
3. **Doji results are polarised**: 5 of 8 never even reached 1R, but of the 3 that did, 2 went on to 3R. A Doji isn't necessarily bad; it just hasn't given an answer yet, so watch whether the next one or two bars follow through.
4. **Doji does poorly mainly because it often follows a weak signal bar**: 5 strong signal bars followed by a Doji averaged +0.20R; all 3 weak signal bars followed by a Doji were stopped out.
5. **The counter side (mild and strong counter bars) has only 5 trades**: too rare to draw conclusions yet.

### Conclusions

1. **The real choice is between 1.5R and 2R, and 1.5R is the best target overall**. Across all 30 trades with no breakeven stop: 1.5R averages +0.48R, 3R +0.43R (optimistic), 1R +0.35R and 2R +0.33R. 1.5R beats 2R because it turns 5 stop-outs or end-of-day exits into wins (+10.53R in total), at the cost of 0.5R less on each of the 12 trades that hit 2R (−6.00R in total). Looking only at strong signal bars, 1R is the worst target (strong pin bar +0.82R, strong trend bar +0.38R) and not worth considering.
2. **The farther the target, the more breakeven helps**: with a 2R target, moving to breakeven at 1R lifts the average from +0.33R to +0.42R per trade; with a 1.5R target, it makes no difference at all (+0.48R either way). The trades it saves, 9/9 ② and 9/29 ②, exactly offset the ones it stops out, 10/1 and 9/23.
3. **3R looks better on paper but is hard to hold**: after 2R there are often deep pullbacks (9/2, 9/14 ②), and reaching 3R often takes half a day or more (9/22, 10/1, 10/8), which goes against every instinct. When the signal bar is big, 2R is already close to the whole day's range (9/3 ②, a 20.75-point stop).
4. **strong trend bars' problem is concentrated in big stops**: splitting the 12 trades in half by stop size, the smaller half (6 points or less) averaged +1.00R and the larger half (8.5 points or more) only +0.06R. With a big stop, a fixed-multiple target gets far away too, and the chart may not have that much room.

### Conclusions

1. **Across all trades, wedge reversal 1st entry is best (+0.81R) and wedge flag in trend 1st entry is worst (+0.09R).**
2. **But strong and weak signal bars aren't spread evenly across the setups**: 4 of the 7 weak signal bars were in wedge flag in trend 1st entry, and wedge flag in trend 2nd entry had none. So flag 1st entry looks bad largely because of its weak signal bars: with strong signal bars only, it's +0.71R, about the same as wedge reversal 2nd entry (+0.80R).
3. **Without the weak signal bars, the setups range only from +0.35R to +1.17R**, with just 5–7 trades each, so I can't say yet which setup is better. Limitations:
   1. Pin bars and trend bars aren't spread evenly either: 5 of the 6 wedge flag in trend 2nd entry trades had a strong trend bar, which may be part of why it's lower;
   2. A few trades had their setup label changed during the review.
4. **Note: once a wedge flag turns into a trading range, it probably loses much of its edge.** A wedge flag's edge comes from the trend before it; if the flag keeps overlapping and slowly becomes a tight trading range, bulls and bears are close to 50/50, and expecting 2R pushes the win rate toward the 33% breakeven line. The data shows a small hint of this: among the wedge flags (1st + 2nd entry), the 5 whose pullback lasted 10 bars or more and that weren't on a trend day all failed to reach 2R; 9/8 and 10/2 both looked "more like a tight trading range" in my review. (Limitations: only 16 trades, far too few; "how much overlap" is a subjective chart read, made after the fact, and may not be clear before entry; pullback length alone doesn't separate them either: the two 9/3 trades had pullbacks of 18 and 19 bars and still hit 2R, because that was a bull trend day.)

## 🧭 Execution rules

I've pulled the conclusions above into 5 rules to trade by from here. Each group has only 6–13 trades, so these are working hypotheses to keep testing with live trades.

<section class="rc policy">
<div class="rc-panel rc-def">

<p class="policy-title">1. Start with the signal bar: whether to trade, and how far to aim</p>

1. **strong pin bar / strong trend bar**: choose a target of 1.5R, 2R or 3R.
2. **weak signal bar**: don't trade it in principle; if you do, keep the target close (1R–1.5R) and take profit when it's hit. All 7 weak signal bars were stopped out, and none of the 11 weak pin bars (experiment + live) reached 2R.

<p class="policy-title">2. Choosing 1.5R, 2R or 3R: read the structure and the market cycle</p>

1. **1.5R**: the 2R level is blocked by a range edge, the start of the rally, or a prior high or low, or the day is range-bound.
2. **2R**: the structure gives enough room with no obvious obstacle; this is the default.
3. **3R**: a trend day, a small stop (signal bar not too big), and you can accept holding for half a day or more.

<p class="policy-title">3. Target of 2R or more: move to breakeven at 1R</p>

1. With a 2R target, moving to breakeven at 1R lifts the average from +0.33R to +0.42R per trade, and full stop-outs (−1R) drop from 14 to 9.
2. With a 1.5R target, breakeven makes no difference (+0.48R either way), so go with your habit.

<p class="policy-title">4. Setup: prefer wedge reversals, be stricter with wedge flags</p>

1. **wedge reversal**: 13 trades averaging +0.53R; +0.98R with strong signal bars only.
2. **wedge flag**: 17 trades averaging +0.18R; +0.55R with strong signal bars only; flag 1st entry alone is 11 trades at just +0.09R.
3. So take a wedge flag only when everything lines up: a strong signal bar, a good location, and a pullback that hasn't turned into a trading range. If it isn't an especially good setup, skip it.

<p class="policy-title">5. Execution discipline</p>

1. If the signal bar fails to trigger twice in a row, cancel the order.
2. No new trades after bar 72.
3. Don't enter in the middle of a range: short near the top of the range, buy near the bottom.

</div>
</section>
