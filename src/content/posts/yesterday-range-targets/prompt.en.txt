I have a CSV of cleaned 1-minute ES futures bars (Databento's ES.c.0 continuous contract, with columns ts_event, instrument_id, open, high, low, close; timestamps in UTC). Please write Python code that measures how many multiples of yesterday's RTH range today's RTH session travels, measured up from yesterday's high and down from yesterday's low. Rules:
1. Convert timestamps to US Eastern time (US/Eastern), handling daylight saving time automatically. Use RTH only, 09:30–16:15, meaning 1-minute bars from 09:30 to 16:14; no overnight session. Each day's open = the open of the 09:30 bar, close = the close of the 16:14 bar, high / low = the highest high / lowest low of the RTH bars.
2. "Yesterday" is the most recent stock market trading day before today, based on the NYSE calendar: pandas' built-in holiday rules plus the unscheduled closures on 2012-10-29, 2012-10-30, 2018-12-05 and 2025-01-09. If that day isn't in my data, or the two days have different instrument_id values, skip today.
3. Yesterday's range R = yesterday's high − yesterday's low. Multiple traveled above = (today's high − yesterday's high) ÷ R; below = (yesterday's low − today's low) ÷ R. Convert prices to whole ticks (× 4, rounded) before comparing, to avoid floating-point errors.
4. Reaching k×: for k = 0 it must be a strict break (at least 1 tick beyond); for k > 0 it must travel ≥ k×. Print the sample size and, for k = 0, 0.5, 0.8, 1 and 2, the share reaching it above, below and on either side. Then check every 0.05× to find the multiple from which "reached below" beats "reached above", and print the share reaching 1× among days that broke above yesterday's high and among days that broke below yesterday's low.
5. By group, print the share reaching 0.5× and 1× above and below, plus the share reaching 0×, 0.5× and 1× on either side:
   - Yesterday up (close > open) / yesterday down;
   - Where yesterday closed in its range, (close − low) ÷ R: top third (≥ 2/3), middle, bottom third (≤ 1/3);
   - Where today opened relative to yesterday's range, (open − yesterday's low) ÷ R: above yesterday's high (> 1), top third, middle, bottom third, below yesterday's low (< 0).
Please add comments to every step explaining why it's done that way.
